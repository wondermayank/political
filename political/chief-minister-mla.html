State Government

Vidhayak (MLA) and Chief Minister

The state mirror of Parliament and the Prime Minister — same logic, smaller scale.

MLA — Member of Legislative Assembly

Also called a Vidhayak. Each state is divided into assembly constituencies; voters directly elect one MLA per constituency, exactly like a Lok Sabha election but at state level.

Chief Minister

The leader of the party/alliance with a majority of MLAs in the state's Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly) — appointed by the Governor on that basis, exactly as the PM is appointed by the President.

Process

From candidate to Chief Minister

Party fields a candidate per constituency

Assembly elections happen state-wide, on a schedule set by the Election Commission.

Voters elect one MLA per constituency

First-Past-The-Post, same as Lok Sabha elections but for the state assembly.

Majority alliance is identified

Whichever party/alliance crosses half the assembly's total seats can form the government.

Governor invites the leader

The Governor appoints that alliance's chosen leader as Chief Minister.

Floor test confirms majority

The new CM must prove majority support in the assembly, usually within days.

Good to know

Where states differ from the Centre

Some states have two houses

A handful of states also have a Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) — an upper house, similar in spirit to the Rajya Sabha, but not every state has one.

President's Rule

If no party can form a stable government, or a constitutional crisis occurs, the Union government can take direct control of a state temporarily.

CM ≠ always an MLA yet

Like the PM, a person can become CM without currently holding a seat, but must get elected to the assembly within six months.
