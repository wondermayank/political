Upper House of Parliament

The Rajya Sabha — the Council of States

Parliament's permanent chamber, built to represent India's states rather than its voters directly.

What it is

The Rajya Sabha represents India's states and union territories, not the population directly. It has up to 245 members: up to 233 elected by state legislative assemblies, plus 12 nominated by the President for expertise in art, science, literature or social service.

Why it's "permanent"

The Rajya Sabha is never fully dissolved. One-third of its members retire every two years and are replaced — giving it continuity even when the Lok Sabha is dissolved for fresh elections.

Process

How a Rajya Sabha member is chosen

Seats allotted by state

Each state gets a number of Rajya Sabha seats roughly based on its population.

MLAs vote, not citizens

Members of that state's Legislative Assembly (MLAs) elect the Rajya Sabha members — not the general public.

Single Transferable Vote

Voting uses proportional representation, so parties with fewer MLAs can still win a seat if they cross the quota.

Six-year term, staggered

Each member serves six years; one-third of the house retires every two years instead of all at once.
