Local Governance

Before the MLA, before the MP — government starts at the village and the ward.

Local self-government in India runs on two tracks: the Panchayati Raj system for rural areas, and Urban Local Bodies for towns and cities. Both are directly elected, and both were given constitutional status by the 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992).

Rural — Panchayati Raj

Three tiers, village to district

01

Gram Panchayat

Village level. Voters elect Panchs (ward members) and a Sarpanch (head) directly — the most local elected office in India.

02

Panchayat Samiti (Block)

Block level. Made up of elected members from a group of Gram Panchayats, coordinating development across the block.

03

Zilla Parishad

District level. The top rural body, overseeing all Panchayat Samitis within a district, chaired by an elected head.

Urban — Local Bodies

Matched to how big the town is

Nagar Panchayat

For areas transitioning from rural to urban — small towns just past village status.

Municipal Council / Nagar Palika

For mid-sized towns and small cities, headed by an elected Chairperson.

Municipal Corporation

For large cities, headed by an elected Mayor, with a Commissioner (an IAS officer) handling administration.
