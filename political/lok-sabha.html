Lower House of Parliament

The Lok Sabha — the House of the People

India's directly elected national chamber. Its majority decides who becomes Prime Minister.

What it is

The Lok Sabha is Parliament's lower house. Its members (MPs) are directly elected by ordinary voters from single-member constituencies across the country — the same way you vote for your local candidate in a general election.

It currently has 543 elected seats, one per constituency, decided by First-Past-The-Post voting: whoever gets the most votes in a constituency wins it, even without a full majority of votes cast.

Why it matters most

Money bills can only start in the Lok Sabha. The Council of Ministers — including the Prime Minister — stays in office only as long as it has the confidence (majority support) of the Lok Sabha.

Its normal term is 5 years, but it can be dissolved earlier by the President on the government's advice, triggering fresh general elections.

Process

How a Lok Sabha election actually works

Constituencies are fixed

India is divided into 543 parliamentary constituencies, each meant to represent roughly similar population.

Candidates file nomination

Party candidates (or independents) file nomination papers with the Election Commission for their constituency.

Campaigning & polling

Voters aged 18+ cast one vote via EVM for a candidate in their own constituency, usually across multiple phases nationally.

Counting & result

The candidate with the most votes in each constituency wins that seat — there is no need for over 50%.

Government formation

The party/alliance crossing 272 seats (a simple majority of 543) is invited to form the government; its leader becomes Prime Minister.
