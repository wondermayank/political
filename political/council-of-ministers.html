Running the government

The Council of Ministers

Once a party wins, it must staff every department — from Defence to Education. Not every minister has equal rank.

Three ranks

Types of ministers, from senior to junior

01

Cabinet Minister

The most senior rank. Heads a major ministry (e.g. Home, Finance, Defence, Education) and sits in Cabinet meetings — the government's top decision-making body.

02

Minister of State (Independent Charge)

Runs a ministry or department independently, with full charge — but does not automatically sit in Cabinet meetings unless invited.

03

Minister of State (MoS)

Assists a Cabinet Minister within a large ministry, usually handling a specific area or set of responsibilities under that senior minister.

How it happens

How ministers — including the Education Minister — are chosen

Prime Minister / Chief Minister selects the team

Ministers must be, or become within six months, a member of the Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, or the equivalent state house.

President / Governor formally appoints

On the advice of the PM (at the Centre) or CM (in a state), the ministers are sworn in.

Portfolios are allocated

The PM/CM decides which minister gets which ministry — for example, who becomes Education Minister — based on experience, seniority, and political balance.

Ministers answer to the legislature

Every minister, including the Education Minister, is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (or state assembly) and can be questioned there.

Example: Ministry of Education

What a ministry like Education actually does

Policy & curriculum

Sets national education policy, curriculum frameworks (through bodies like NCERT), and standards for schools and universities.

Regulating institutions

Oversees regulatory bodies for higher education and technical education, and coordinates with state education departments.

Center-state coordination

Education is a 'concurrent' subject — both the Union Education Minister and each state's Education Minister hold responsibility over it together.

The same pattern — Cabinet Minister, MoS, and department — repeats for every ministry: Home Affairs, Finance, Defence, External Affairs, Health, and dozens more, each with its own Cabinet Minister and supporting Ministers of State.
