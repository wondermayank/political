Heads of State

President & Governor — elected without a public vote

India's most indirect elections: chosen entirely by other elected representatives, not citizens.

President of India

Elected by an Electoral College made up of all elected MPs (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha) and all elected MLAs of every state and union territory — never by citizens directly.

Each vote is weighted: an MLA's vote value depends on their state's population and assembly size, so every state's voice is proportionally represented.

Governor of a State

Unlike the President, a Governor is not elected at all — appointed directly by the President (on the Union government's advice) to represent the Centre in each state, for a term usually of five years.

Process

How the President is elected

Electoral College assembles

All elected MPs and all elected MLAs across India form the voting body — nominated members do not vote.

Value of each vote is calculated

A formula based on state population and number of assembly seats gives MLA votes their weight; MP votes are weighted to balance both houses.

Single Transferable Vote, secret ballot

Voters rank candidates by preference rather than choosing just one.

Winner needs a quota, not just top votes

A candidate must cross 50% of valid votes value, counted through preference transfers if needed.

Term & role

Serves five years as ceremonial head of state, supreme commander of the armed forces, and final assent-giver on laws.

What they actually do

Ceremonial, but not powerless

President

Appoints the PM and Governors, gives assent to bills, can return non-money bills for reconsideration, and acts as final authority during a constitutional crisis.

Governor

Appoints the Chief Minister of the state on the same majority principle, can reserve state bills for the President, and plays a key role if no party has a clear majority.
