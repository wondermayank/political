Who fields the candidates

Political Parties & Elections

Every seat on the political ladder is filled by someone a party chose to run — here's how that decision is made.

How a party is formed

Any group of citizens can register a political party with the Election Commission of India by submitting a constitution, membership details, and an affidavit committing to the Indian Constitution and principles of democracy.

Recognition levels

A new party starts as a registered unrecognised party. It can grow into a State Party or National Party by crossing vote-share or seat thresholds in elections — recognition brings a reserved symbol and other privileges.

Candidate selection

How a party picks who gets the ticket

Internal screening

State and district party units shortlist potential candidates based on local standing, past performance, or public profile.

Central/state election committee decides

A party's top decision-making body reviews shortlists constituency by constituency.

Winnability & alliances weighed

Parties often prioritise candidates seen as likely to win, and adjust picks to accommodate alliance partners.

Ticket (Form B) issued

The chosen candidate is formally authorised to contest under the party's symbol.

Nomination filed with Election Commission

The candidate files papers, an affidavit of assets/criminal record, and a security deposit.

The election itself

Who runs it, and how it's decided

Election Commission of India

An independent constitutional body that conducts, supervises, and ensures fairness of all elections — from Panchayat to President.

EVMs & VVPAT

Votes are cast on Electronic Voting Machines, verified by a paper trail (VVPAT) that voters can visually confirm.

First-Past-The-Post

In most Indian elections, the candidate with the most votes wins the seat — a simple majority of votes cast isn't required.
