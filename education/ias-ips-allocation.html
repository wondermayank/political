The formula behind the badge

How IAS and IPS are decided

There's no separate exam for IAS vs IPS — the same UPSC CSE rank decides both, through preference and vacancy.

The allocation logic: for each rank, going from 1st downward, UPSC checks that candidate's preferred service order and allots the highest-preference service that still has an open vacancy for their category. A rank 1 candidate who prefers IAS almost always gets it; a candidate further down the list gets whichever of their preferred services still has a seat left.

Where officers land

IAS vs IPS vs IFS — what each one does

IAS — Indian Administrative Service

General administration: District Magistrate, Secretary-level policy roles, and running government departments. Widely seen as the most sought-after service.

IPS — Indian Police Service

Leads the police force: starts as Assistant/Deputy Superintendent of Police, rising to Superintendent, IG, and Director General of Police.

IFS — Indian Foreign Service

Represents India abroad: diplomats, embassy officials, and eventually ambassadors and high commissioners.

After allocation

Training before the first posting

01

Foundation Course

All selected officers, regardless of service, train together first at LBSNAA (Mussoorie) to build shared understanding across services.

02

Service-specific training

IAS officers continue at LBSNAA; IPS officers move to SVPNPA (Hyderabad) for police-specific training; other services train at their own academies.

03

District training / attachment

Trainee officers are attached to a working district to learn hands-on before independent charge.

04

First posting

IAS officers typically start as Assistant Collector/SDM; IPS officers start as Assistant Superintendent of Police (ASP).
