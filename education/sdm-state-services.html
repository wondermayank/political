The state's own ladder

SDM & State Civil Services

Not every officer is UPSC-selected — states run their own, parallel exam and promotion ladder.

What an SDM is

A Sub-Divisional Magistrate heads a sub-division (a group of blocks within a district) — handling law and order, revenue matters, and local administration. It's usually the first independent-charge posting for a young officer.

Two roads to the SDM's desk

A fresh IAS officer (via UPSC) is often posted as SDM early in their career. Separately, a State PCS officer (via a state's own exam) can also rise to SDM-equivalent rank through years of service and promotion.

State PCS ladder

How a State Public Service Commission exam works

Prelims

An objective screening test, similar in structure to UPSC Prelims but focused on the state's own syllabus and current affairs.

Mains

Descriptive papers testing depth of knowledge, including state-specific history, geography, and administration.

Interview

A personality test assessing suitability for state administrative and police roles.

Allocation by rank

Top-ranked candidates are allotted to the State Administrative Service (leading toward SDM, then District Magistrate); others go to State Police Service (leading toward DSP) or allied services.

Career growth

From SDM upward

01

Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM)

First independent-charge posting — a sub-division within a district.

02

Additional District Magistrate

Assists the DM across a full district's administration.

03

District Magistrate / Collector

Heads the entire district's civil administration — the most visible officer at district level.

04

Divisional Commissioner / Secretary

Oversees multiple districts, or moves into state-level policy roles in the secretariat.
