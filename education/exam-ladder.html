The Exam Ladder

Dozens of exams sound similar. Here's how they actually stack, from school to the UPSC.

Stage 1

School: the eligibility gate

Class 10

Board exam that decides the stream — Science, Commerce, or Arts/Humanities — for the next two years.

Class 12

Board exam that sets eligibility for entrance exams and, for most government jobs later, isn't checked again — only the degree matters.

Stage 2

Graduation: the real minimum qualification

A bachelor's degree in any subject, from a recognised university, is the minimum requirement for UPSC, most State PCS exams, SSC CGL, and Banking exams. What you studied matters far less than most people expect — an engineer, a historian, and a commerce graduate can all sit the same exam.

Stage 3

Competitive exams, by what they lead to

UPSC Civil Services Exam (CSE)

National-level. Leads to IAS, IPS, IFS and 20+ Central Services. The most competitive exam in the country.

Read more

State PCS

State-level equivalent of UPSC CSE. Leads to SDM, DSP and other state-cadre officer posts.

Read more

SSC (CGL, CHSL, etc.)

Staff Selection Commission exams for central government clerical, inspector, and assistant-level posts.

Banking exams (IBPS, SBI)

For probationary officer and clerical posts in public sector banks — a separate but similarly structured ladder.

Judicial Services Exam

State-level exam for entry into the judiciary as a Civil Judge — a separate ladder from the administrative services.

Defence exams (NDA, CDS)

Lead into the armed forces via the National Defence Academy or Combined Defence Services exam.
